## 1) Waking in the circle

> *"The player awakes in a large sewer surrounded by the skeletal remains of a
> cult lets say a cult trying to summon a being from the astral."*

Act 0 is two pages now — waking, and Knowledge saying *"Get up… The water is
going somewhere and so should you."* The other six pages did not get deleted;
they became the six tidbits below, which is the same writing delivered at the
moment it is useful instead of before the player has anything to use it on.

The player then wakes in the ritual circle among **thirteen bone piles**, in a
new interior room, `The Undercity`.

**Two new tile ids:** `TILE_FLOOR_SEWER = 16` and `TILE_SEWER_WATER = 17`. The
floor id joins `FLOOR_IDS`; the channel joins `INTERIOR_IDS` but deliberately
**not** `FLOOR_IDS`, because it is not walkable — `isWaterTile` now answers
true for 17, which is what makes the channel impassable through the collision
path everything else already uses.

`_stampInteriorBand` paints one floor id per room, which is right for a room
that is a rectangle. The sewer is not, so rooms may now carry an optional
`tileAt(tx, ty)` hook and the sewer's returns void for `#`, water for `~` and
floor for everything else. That is the whole extension: one `if`.

## 2) A maze, and water that tells the truth

> *"A maze of tunnels, but if the player follows the water they go the right
> direction."*

The map is ASCII in `src/data/sewer.js` — you can move a wall by typing a
character, and `sewerFaults()` checks the result. But the **first draft** of it
could not sensibly be typed, because the design rests on a property of the whole
layout rather than of any one tile. So it is generated by
`tools/build_round82_sewer.py` and pasted in as data.

The maze is a spanning tree over a 7×5 grid of chambers, so there is exactly one
route between any two of them, and the channel is laid along the unique
start→exit path. Water beside a corridor therefore *means* on-route, everywhere,
because the tree has no second route for it to be wrong about.

**The generator got this wrong twice, and both are worth recording:**

- **A depth-first carve gave a snake.** 29 of 35 chambers were on the one route
  and there were six dead ends — so "follow the water" was a signpost pointing
  down the only corridor there was. Randomised **Prim's** grows the tree outward
  from the start instead of tunnelling, and gives **11 route chambers and 24
  dead ends**: a maze with real choices, and therefore a signpost with a real
  job.
- **The channel flooded one edge of every route chamber** and the map came back
  more water than floor. The player only asks the question at a junction, so the
  water now runs through the route's doorways and two tiles either side, and
  nowhere else. Fifty water tiles in a map of 1,312.

The fault checker rebuilds the chamber graph from the ASCII and asserts the
actual promise — *following the water reaches the cultist*, and *the watered
doorways are a single path*. Its first version asserted that the channel was one
connected body, which was a fact about the first draft rather than a
requirement.

Census: `{tiles:1312, water:50, tidbits:6, weapons:8, armour:8, potions:8,
essences:8, slimes:17, bones:13}`.

## 2.1) Knowledge, slimes, things on the floor, and a man in the last room

Every chamber that is **not** on the route is a dead end with loot in it. That
is the other half of the deal and the reason exploring pays: the route is safe
and empty, the branches are where the sword is.

**Knowledge's six beats** fire on proximity, once each, in his own name and
colour: attacks, essences, potions and awakening stones, gear, and the world.
**Seventeen slime packs**, two of them on the route so the attack lesson has a
target.

**The cultist is Sereth Vane of The Unmade** — one of the ten authored cults,
not a bespoke antagonist. *"They believe the world is a mistake with a
correction available"*, so the thing they were pulling through is the
correction. 46 HP, 4 damage, and he is the simplest hostile in the game because
the player fighting him has no essences, no abilities and whatever they picked
up off the floor. He speaks three lines, then attacks on his own.

His blows go through `_monsterHitPlayer` on a small shim carrying the eight
fields it reads, so they are dodged, blocked, resisted and thorned like
everything else rather than subtracting from HP directly.

He **always** drops a Rare-or-better essence — a floor on a real roll from the
real catalogue, not a scripted stand-in.

## 2.2) Out, and the Guildmaster

The ladder is only offered once he is down. Before that it is a grate that will
not shift, and the game says so rather than ignoring the press.

Climbing out puts the player at the original spawn point in Cadence, where
Guildmaster Yorin is waiting, having been told. He explains the Adventure
Society and gives one random essence.

**He no longer hands out a full set of armour and weapons.**
`_grantStarterArmor` is still in the file and is now called by nothing; the
player is dressed in what they found in the sewer.

## 3) Loot on the floor

> *"coins should be picked up automatically but everything else should drop in a
> standard ARPG format where the player can choose to pick the items up."*

Until this round exactly one thing in the game ever touched the ground: a coin
pip. Everything else — essences, stones, parts, potions, gear, weapons — was
pushed straight into the backpack at the moment of the kill and announced with
float text ending in "(bag)". **Six** separate call sites did their own
`inventory.<kind>.push(...)`, which is why `_rollSiteFind` carried a comment
explaining that it granted directly because *"there is no world-pickup path for
essences and stones"*. There is one now, and that comment is gone with the code
it described.

`src/data/loot.js` is the contract. One drop type with a `kind` discriminator
rather than six parallel paths, because drawing an icon, colouring a label by
rarity, lying on the floor and being picked up are the same job for a potion and
for a legendary sword. The only thing that differs is which container it lands
in, and that is **one switch** in `grantLootToPlayer` — `ownedWeapons` is a Set
and everything else is an array, exactly the asymmetry that gets copied wrong
when it is written out six times.

Two radii that had been the same constant by accident are now separate, because
they answer different questions:

- `LOOT_MAGNET_RADIUS = 24` — how far a **coin** is vacuumed in from. A coin is
  taken *from* you walking past.
- `LOOT_REACH = 46` — how far an **item** offers itself. Matched to
  `NPC_INTERACT_RADIUS` on purpose: picking a sword up and talking to somebody
  are the same gesture, so they should feel the same.

`LOOT_TTL_SECONDS = 0` — drops lie there forever. A game that deletes the sword
you were walking back for is worse than one whose floor gets untidy.

## What the screenshot pass caught that the suite could not

The suite proves the prologue **joins up**. It cannot prove it is
lookable-at, and two things were wrong in the frames:

- **The cultist was a purple ellipse.** `artKey: 'npc_cultist_a'` named a
  texture that does not exist, and the fallback drew a blob. He is
  `cultistArtKey('void', 1)` now, and his sprite takes its anchor and scale from
  the `NPC_ART` entry the way `_buildNpcs` does — a hand-picked origin sinks
  some models into the floor and floats others above it.
- **The way out was an unmarked tile.** "Press E to climb out" appeared on bare
  flagstone. There is no ladder or grate in the asset packs, so it is drawn: a
  lit diamond on the floor and a shaft of daylight narrowing upward. It is the
  only light down there that is not lamp-coloured, and the player has spent
  eight minutes looking for it.

## The regression, and one real fault it found

**95/95 suites recorded. No new failures.** The five that fail
(`round41` ×3, `round43` ×3, `round45` ×1, `round48_agentA`, `round5_essence`)
all failed identically at round 81 and are carried, not caused.

Getting there took three fixes, and the first is the interesting one:

**Ninety suites were teleporting their player into the town while the scene
still believed it was inside a room.** They all open a new game and call
`_endAct0()`, which since this round drops the player into the sewer. Inside a
room, `_collidesObstacle` consults only `interiorSolids`, and monster ticking and
projectile resolution change with it — so round 44's homing bolt resolved on
nothing and reported `damaged:false`. It looked like a projectile bug and was a
location bug.

The fix is `_skipPrologue()`, and it is **not** a test backdoor: it sets
`player.sewerDone`, which is the same flag the shipped game sets when the player
climbs the ladder. A player who has done the prologue does not do it again,
whether they earned that by playing it or by loading a save. All 91 call sites
now name it, and the prologue itself is covered end to end by `test_round82`.

Two suites were asserting things that this round legitimately changed, and both
were re-specified in the data rather than by naming the sewer in the suite:

- **`round46` — "every interior still finds its exterior."** The sewer is the
  first interior in the game with no building above it, so `SEWER_ROOM` declares
  `noExterior: true` and the suite skips rooms that say so. A future roofless
  room excludes itself by describing itself.
- **`round43` — "every room stands on real floor tiles."** This was
  `t>=8&&t<=10`, a literal written when there were three interior floors, sampled
  at one fixed tile — which works only for a room whose floor is a solid
  rectangle. It asks `isInteriorFloor` now and samples across the room. The
  property is unchanged; the way it is measured had expired.

One thing worth knowing for next round: **the shared-browser lane serves cached
ES modules**, so a source edit made mid-slice is not necessarily what ran.
`run_one.sh <suite>` without `--shared` launches a fresh browser and is the
honest check after an edit.

## `test_round82` — 28/28

It walks the whole prologue rather than checking that the pieces exist. A
prologue that has every part and does not join up is exactly the failure this
shape of test catches and a parts inventory does not.

Data faults · Act 0 opens · the player wakes in the circle among the bones ·
438 wall colliders · 32 drops · 17 slime packs · every sampled channel tile
impassable · all six tidbits fire once each in Knowledge's name and never twice ·
a drop offers itself with its name on the floor · taking a weapon arms you · the
cultist speaks, says what they were summoning, then attacks · a starting
character kills him in 8 swings · he always drops Rare-or-better · the ladder ·
the spawn point · the Adventure Society · one essence · **no armour** · and
nothing twice.

The one thing simulated is the player's legs — walking the maze at the game's
own speed would take eight minutes of wall clock. Everything else is the shipped
code doing its own work.
